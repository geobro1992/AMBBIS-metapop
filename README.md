## Code for: Brooks, G.C., A.K. Martin, C.A. Haas, and H.C. Chandler. 2026. Prioritizing habitat restoration efforts for amphibian metapopulations

Habitat restoration is one of the primary tools that conservationists use for species recovery. For species that function in metapopulations, it can be challenging for land managers with limited resources to identify cost-effective restoration strategies. Here, we determine restoration priorities at two sites in Florida and Georgia within the range of the endangered reticulated flatwoods salamander (_Ambystoma bishopi_). Our objectives were to identify wetland patches with the largest contributions to metapopulation capacity and determine the optimal restoration scenarios for given levels of investment. We used remotely-sensed data to identify wetlands that could provide reticulated flatwoods salamander breeding habitat and created vegetation height classes to distinguish existing from potential breeding habitat. For both existing and potential habitat patches, we identified those that contributed the most to metapopulation capacity and explored restoration scenarios involving different combinations of wetlands and areas restored. Fully restoring the landscapes increased the number of pairwise connections between wetlands within reticulated flatwoods salamander dispersal distances threefold at the Florida site and twofold at the Georgia site. Given finite resources, the best supported scenario at the Florida site favored a combined strategy of increasing habitat within existing patches and creating habitat in potential patches, whereas the best supported scenario at the Georgia site favored only increasing habitat within existing patches. Our results revealed differences in the most cost-effective restoration strategy at two landscapes driven by differences in the spatial configuration of wetlands and the degree of site degradation. For large, complex landscapes with myriad potential restoration options, an analytical approach can help to ensure conservation efforts yield the greatest benefit. This work provides insights to expedite reticulated flatwood salamander recovery efforts. When devising restoration activities, managers must account for the current state of the landscape, the networks of existing and potential patches, and the dispersal ecology of the focal species. Our approach can be generalized to any landscape within the reticulated flatwoods salamander range to aid restoration efforts, as well as adapted for the management of other imperiled amphibians. This work adds to the growing examples of prioritizing process-based conservation and highlights the utility of metapopulation theory to inform species’ recovery.
